What we learned.

Three findings make the rest of this plan inevitable.

We reviewed five competitors, two paid social audits, and a full local SEO read of Bunkr's footprint inside the Perimeter. The research kept circling back to the same three things.

01
The category is split into two lanes.
Five Iron, Fair Way Social, and Atlanta Golf & Social sell golf as a social outing. Back Nine and Golf Envy sell tech and performance credentials. Bunkr's brand sits in Lane A. The current paid social spend is built for Lane B. We are advertising to a different audience than the one the brand is for.
02
The Perimeter paid social space is empty.
Atlanta Golf & Social has 213 Facebook page likes and almost no paid activity. Back Nine is just landing. Fair Way Social is in Alpharetta with a multi-sport story that dilutes its golf identity. Inside the Perimeter, nobody owns paid social. Whoever moves first claims it.
03
Nobody is doing retargeting.
Every cold-ad visitor who does not convert on the first click is being abandoned by every local competitor. Retargeting is a same-week unlock and nobody in our market is doing it.

We win by owning the local funnel and the local search anchor. Then Bunkr's team builds on top.

Who we're going after.

Three audiences. Three messages. Three buying behaviors. Not one broad campaign aimed at "golfers." Each ICP gets its own ad set, its own creative, and its own conversion path inside the paid social funnel.

01 · Primary

The Dunwoody Native

28 to 55. Already a golfer. Walking or 5-minute-drive distance. The membership engine of this business. Becomes a regular. Brings friends. Reached through GBP, local search, and tight-radius paid social.

"Your place. Two minutes from your driveway."
02 · Primary

The Perimeter Commuter

25 to 45. Works inside the Perimeter at State Farm, Cox, Mercedes-Benz HQ, or the office towers along Ashford-Dunwoody and Perimeter Center. Kills two hours after 5pm instead of sitting on 285. After-work leagues, date-night-before-the-drive-home, corporate team outings.

"Five minutes from your office. Two hours of fun. Then home."
03 · Primary

The Crew Planner

25 to 45. Books for the group. Birthday, bachelorette, bachelor, corporate party. Not necessarily a golfer. The TopGolf-but-closer audience. The highest-revenue, highest-volume use case for the bays.

"We host the night. You just show up."

Channel one. Meta paid social.

A real funnel. Three layers. Three ICPs.

The account today is 100% conversion targeting 2.6M to 3M cold people inside a 15-mile radius. We are flipping it. Three layers, three audiences, three creative tracks. Each ICP gets its own ad set. The geography tightens. The retargeting runs.

Layer 01 · Awareness
25-30% of spend
Fill the retargeting pool.
Vertical video. Facility tours, Trackman moments, group fun. Tight 10-mile geo around Dunwoody. Advantage+ audience expansion off. The goal here is not bookings. It is reach and ThruPlay.
KPI · Cost per ThruPlay, video view rate
Layer 02 · Consideration
20-25% of spend
Move warm audiences down the path.
Retargets video viewers and page engagers. Membership explainers, "first time at a simulator" content, event recaps. The goal here is landing page views and warm intent.
KPI · Cost per landing page view
Layer 03 · Conversion
50% of spend
Convert hot, not cold.
Bay bookings and membership signups. Hot retargeting only. 14 to 30-day website visitors, booking-page abandoners. Hard CTAs, urgency, first-session offers. Not broad cold targeting.
KPI · Cost per booking
Fix First

The pixel problem we have to solve before we scale.

The Meta pixel is on Bunkr's main domain. The booking flow lives on yourgolfbooking.com. Today, we cannot track post-click conversions. Every dollar we spend on paid social is partially blind. Either we move the pixel onto the booking domain or we move the booking flow onto Bunkr's domain. The whole paid plan rests on this.

What changes from day one.

Geography tightens. 15-mile radius drops to 10. Advantage+ audience expansion turns off.
Audiences split. Three ad sets (Native, Commuter, Crew Planner), not one broad campaign.
Retargeting goes live. Website visitors (14 and 30 day windows), video viewers, page engagers.
Creative goes vertical. 9:16 video as the primary format. Static used only when offer clarity matters more than vibe.

Creative direction. The look.

Moodboard first. Mockups second.

Every paid ad has to live on a feed next to organic posts. So the creative direction is built to feel native to Instagram and Meta first, polished second. Lifestyle-forward, golf-coded, anti-country-club. Bunkr's voice, not an agency's.

Pillar 01

Lifestyle, not sport.

Golf is the backdrop. The story is the people, the night, the crew, the bar, the bay. The swing comes second.

Pillar 02

Local, not corporate.

Dunwoody specific. Perimeter specific. Independent and named, not a franchise. The brand should feel like a neighbor, not a chain.

Pillar 03

Native, not produced.

Vertical, fast, phone-ready. Every static and video should pass the "could this be organic" test before it ships as paid.

The moodboard.

The vibe target. Country-club energy crossed with neighborhood bar warmth. Trackman, leather, brass, denim, dim light. Where the brand lives.

Channel two. Google Business Profile.

The local search anchor. We own this.

GBP is the most underused asset Bunkr has. It is claimed, but underbuilt. No primary category locked, few photos, no Q&A seeded, no services listed, no posts. Underneath it, the site has broken footer NAP and no schema. All of that is fixable, and the local pack is winnable.

The Goal
Top 3in the local pack
"Indoor golf Dunwoody." "Golf simulator Atlanta Perimeter." "Golf simulator near me" inside the Perimeter. Owning the local pack for these three searches is the GBP outcome we're optimizing for.
01
Categories locked.
Primary: Indoor golf course. Secondaries: Sports bar, Event venue, Recreation center. Each secondary is a separate ranking signal.

Bonus 01 · For Bunkr's team

The Founder Playbook.

What Bunkr's team owns. The behaviors that compound on top.

Marketwake delivers the paid funnel and the local search anchor above. This section is the work that sits outside our scope but determines whether the rest compounds or evaporates. Three behaviors, one review goal, and one rule. None of it is glamorous. All of it is owner-led.

01

Film constantly.

Five Iron pays M&C Saatchi agency money to look authentic. The local counter to that is being authentic. Turn the camera around. Walk the space. Talk about Tuesday's league, the new menu item, what just happened in bay 3.

  • Walkthrough Reels, 60 seconds, no script.
  • Member spotlights with real names.
  • Event hype: Masters, Ryder Cup, watch parties.
  • Trackman moments. Member crushes one. Post it.
  • Before/after of the space. New bay. New decor. New menu.
Six days a week
02

Push reviews. Owner-led.

Reviews are not a marketing task. They are a hospitality task. At the end of every session, the staff or the founders ask. QR code at every bay landing on the GBP review link. One-sentence script.

  • "If you had a good time, the biggest thing you can do for us is leave a Google review. Takes 30 seconds."
  • 25 reviews in the first 30 days.
  • 8 to 10 per month after.
  • QR cards printed, mounted, staff trained on the ask.
The local search lever
03

Be the face. People buy from people.

AI search and local search both reward named, real, quotable owners. Ashton and Jonl are real. Use that. The franchises cannot say "the owner bartends here." Bunkr can.

  • Founder bios on the site.
  • Founder voice in the GBP Q&A.
  • Founder face in two-thirds of the Reels.
  • Founder quotes anywhere the brand shows up in press.
The local moat

The single biggest lever in local search.

Reviews compound. Each one makes the next one easier to earn, harder for competitors to catch, and more useful as a signal to Google. The 100 number is the goal. The velocity is what matters.

QR card at every bay. Asked at the end of every session. Owner-led when possible. This is a hospitality habit before it is a marketing tactic.

One thing we do not chase. Production value.

The brands that look polished on social cost real money to look that way. The brands that look real on social win this category locally. Phone, daylight, talk and walk. That is the entire production direction.
